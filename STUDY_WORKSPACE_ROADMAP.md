# Study Workspace Roadmap

## Purpose

The Study Workspace is the long-term evolution of the Study Drawer. The goal is to make every verse and chapter feel like a connected study environment instead of forcing users to hunt through separate menus.

Scripture remains first. Every resource supports the Bible text.

## Core Experience

For a verse or chapter, the workspace should gather:

- KJV text
- commentaries
- cross references
- Webster 1828 definitions
- Strong's definitions
- historical and cultural background
- maps and places
- people
- timeline
- audio teaching
- video teaching
- personal notes
- highlights
- sermon outline
- journal links
- prayer links
- export options

## Important Boundary

The workspace may include an AI study assistant later, but it must be clearly distinguished from source materials. AI should never appear as Scripture, commentary, doctrinal authority, or a replacement for reviewed resources.

## Phase 1: Passage Study

For each chapter:

- chapter summary
- key verse
- main themes
- key words
- cross references
- recommended commentaries
- related books
- background
- people and places
- applications
- discussion questions
- sermon starter

## Phase 2: Commentary And Doctrine Explorer

For key passages:

- show selected trusted commentaries side by side
- show author perspective notes
- show agreement areas
- show differences and cautions
- send insight to sermon notes
- send insight to journal

Example passage:

- John 3:16

Possible authors:

- H. A. Ironside
- J. Vernon McGee
- Lewis Sperry Chafer
- C. I. Scofield
- Charles Spurgeon

Only show authors where the exact resource is available, reviewed, and rights-cleared.

## Phase 3: Sermon And Teaching Database

For each Bible book and passage, connect:

- audio
- video
- books
- commentaries
- charts
- maps
- outlines
- illustrations
- questions
- applications

This should help a Sunday School teacher or pastor move from reading to teaching without leaving the study flow.

## Phase 4: Resource Timeline

Show where authors fit historically.

Example:

| Years | Author |
| --- | --- |
| 1834-1892 | Charles Spurgeon |
| 1843-1921 | C. I. Scofield |
| 1851-1945 | Arno C. Gaebelein |
| 1876-1952 | Lewis Sperry Chafer |
| 1876-1951 | H. A. Ironside |
| 1904-1988 | J. Vernon McGee |

The timeline should help users understand historical context before comparing authors.

## Phase 5: Topic Index

Users should be able to search a topic such as "Faith" and find:

- Bible passages
- books
- sermons
- videos
- articles
- illustrations
- biographies
- maps or places if relevant
- applications

Starter topics:

- Faith
- Grace
- Prayer
- Judgment
- Repentance
- Salvation
- Church
- Missions
- Family
- Evangelism
- Prophecy
- Christian living

## Phase 6: Teaching Series

Create curated paths for different users.

### New Believer

- Gospel of John
- Romans
- assurance
- prayer
- church

### Sunday School Teacher

- lesson preparation
- cross references
- outlines
- illustrations
- historical background

### Pastor

- sermon builder
- word study tools
- maps
- quotes
- illustrations
- public-domain commentaries

## Success Standard

A user should be able to open a passage, study it for an hour, and feel that the app helped them reach useful, reviewed, Bible-centered material faster than a broad general-purpose library.
