# Public Reveal And Video Plan

Reviewed: August 16, 2026

## The Reason To Choose It

Father's Business Bible Study should not introduce itself as a smaller replacement for Logos, e-Sword, SwordSearcher, Olive Tree, or Blue Letter Bible. Those products already have established libraries, study systems, and loyal users.

The stronger promise is:

> Read. Understand. Obey. Teach.

Supporting sentence:

> A fast, KJV-first path from the words of Scripture to trusted study, a faithful response, and a lesson, sermon, or presentation.

This gives two honest reasons to use the app:

1. A Christian who does not own serious Bible software can begin with a focused, affordable study path instead of assembling modules and learning a dense research system.
2. A pastor, teacher, missionary, or student who already uses other software can add Father's Business as the simpler place to turn finished research into prayer, obedience, teaching notes, sermons, and church presentation.

Do not compete on the number of books. Compete on how quickly a person can complete a meaningful Bible-centered task without losing the passage.

## The Signature Workflow

Call the public workflow **Study To Teach**. It is not a new feature screen; it is the connected path the existing features must prove.

1. Read the KJV passage in context.
2. Tap a word and see reviewed Webster 1828, Strong's, occurrence, and source information together.
3. Follow TSK and passage-linked resources without losing the original passage.
4. Compare a small number of clearly identified commentaries.
5. Record observation, doctrine, application, prayer, and a specific act of obedience.
6. Reuse the saved work as a lesson, sermon, handout, memory card, or presentation.
7. Export the user's work in a durable format.

The app becomes memorable when this entire path is faster than switching among a Bible app, dictionary, commentary site, notes app, word processor, and slide program.

## What Makes The Promise Credible

- **KJV-first clarity:** the Bible remains visible and every quoted Scripture uses KJV wording.
- **One-tap Word Lens:** Webster 1828, Strong's, English occurrences, and first use answer the first word-study question together.
- **Passage-linked depth:** TSK, commentary, books, maps, archaeology, sermons, hymns, quotes, and personal notes appear because they relate to the current passage.
- **Study that reaches life and ministry:** observation leads to doctrine, prayer, obedience, a lesson, a sermon, or slides instead of ending in a list of search results.
- **Source trust:** author, edition, source, rights, review status, and doctrinal cautions remain visible.
- **A curated library:** reviewed resources are easier to use than an unmanaged pile of modules.
- **Companion value:** exports and portable user data allow the app to work beside a user's existing tools instead of trapping the user.

These distinctions answer the competitor landscape without making unsupported comparison claims. Logos offers extensive guides and sermon tools; Olive Tree connects a passage to a user's library; SwordSearcher emphasizes fast search, deep referencing, and verse guides. Father's Business must therefore demonstrate its complete KJV **study-to-obedience-to-teaching** path, not merely announce that it also has dictionaries and commentaries.

## Reveal Principle

Show one real result before showing a feature list.

The first public proof should answer:

> Can this help me understand one KJV passage and prepare something faithful in less time?

Use Hosea 4 as the first demonstration because commentary delivery for that chapter has already been measured and the teaching workflow has an existing QA history. A second demonstration can use John 3 for broad familiarity.

Every published demonstration must:

- use a production build or release candidate, not a mockup
- show the actual wait time rather than hiding it with edits
- use only public, reviewed resources
- keep admin and acquisition screens out of view
- avoid unverified claims such as "complete Strong's," "complete TSK," or "every public-domain book"
- identify the app as private beta, coming soon, or free public beta according to its actual state
- avoid announcing a calendar date until the release candidate has passed the launch gates

## Coming Soon Gate

Do not publish a coming-soon campaign until all of these are true:

- `/` first-load JavaScript is below the 1.5 MB gzip beta milestone
- the current chapter, Word Lens, TSK preview, commentary, notes, and export paths pass at 390px, tablet, and desktop widths
- signed-out and ordinary signed-in users cannot reach admin, rights, import, permission, or acquisition tools
- a brand-new user can complete read, word lookup, save, and export without coaching
- the five core workflows produce no browser errors or unhandled requests in a production build
- feedback and resource-correction reports reach a monitored inbox or queue
- every resource visible in a recording has source and rights evidence
- screenshots and recordings contain no personal email, account, permission, or private-note data

The app now clears the 1.5 MB coming-soon bundle gate at approximately 0.70 MB gzip for `/`. The exact 31,102-verse KJV corpus loads separately and still needs real-device timing before public promotion.

## Public Beta Gate

Do not open the free public beta until the Coming Soon gate passes and:

- at least 5-10 trusted testers complete the Study To Teach task
- critical tester failures are fixed and the same task is rerun
- backup and one full restore are tested
- export and account-deletion instructions are accurate
- privacy, terms, support, copyright, and correction paths are public
- production environment variables and admin allowlists are verified
- the public library audit reports no permission-needed, personal-use-only, or admin-review items
- the launch owner can support normal questions and correction requests consistently

## Release Stages

### Stage 1: Quiet Proof

- Finish performance, Word Lens, admin protection, mobile QA, and persistence.
- Record Video 0 repeatedly as a usability test.
- Use short private clips in permission conversations when helpful.
- Invite only trusted testers.

### Stage 2: Coming Soon

- Publish Videos 1 and 2.
- Open a no-payment beta interest form.
- Share one workflow result each week rather than daily feature announcements.
- Say `coming soon to a limited free beta`; do not publish a date.

### Stage 3: Limited Free Beta

- Admit small groups in waves.
- Publish Videos 3-5 and a visible known-limitations page.
- Measure whether users complete and repeat the Study To Teach workflow.
- Keep optional support separate from access.

### Stage 4: Sustainable Public Product

- Add paid workflow value only after retention, sync, support, backups, and rights operations are dependable.
- Keep KJV reading and reviewed public-domain core study free.
- License books and media under written agreements rather than implying that a subscription grants rights.

## Success Measures

The reveal is working when people can repeat the reason for the app without being shown a feature list.

Track:

- percentage of invited testers who complete read, word lookup, save, and teaching output
- median time to complete that path
- week-one and week-four return rate
- number of testers who prepare a real lesson or sermon
- support and correction volume per active tester
- interest-list to activated-tester conversion
- percentage of users who describe the app as a study-to-teach tool in their own words

Do not optimize for video views alone. The proof is repeated, faithful use.
