# Bible Study App Sustainability Research

Research reviewed: August 16, 2026

This report turns current Bible software research into a practical product and funding plan for Father's Business Bible Study. Current prices are snapshots, not permanent facts; confirm them again before publishing pricing or approving a budget.

## Executive Recommendation

Launch a narrow, free public beta first. Keep the KJV Bible and core public-domain study tools free. Ask for optional support while measuring whether people return weekly and complete meaningful study. Add a paid study tier only after account sync, export, backups, support, and rights controls are dependable.

The strongest position is not "another enormous digital library." It is:

> A fast, KJV-first path from reading a verse to understanding its words and context, recording an obedient response, and preparing faithful teaching.

The app can eventually earn revenue from workflow value, licensed books, church tools, and original products without charging people merely to read Scripture or access unrestricted public-domain material.

## What Successful Products Prove

### Logos: depth, integration, and professional value

[Logos subscriptions](https://www.logos.com/configure/subscriptions) currently range from $9.99 to $19.99 per month, with lower effective annual rates. Its enduring strengths are a large licensed library, passage-linked research, original-language study, sermon preparation, and professional workflows. Logos also serves institutions and reports millions of users through its [education program](https://www.logos.com/remote-theological-study-library).

Lesson: pastors and serious students will pay when many trusted resources become one efficient research system. The app should learn from the integration, not copy the complexity or catalog size.

### Accordance: speed and ownership

[Accordance](https://www.accordancebible.com/shop/) combines one-time starter purchases, paid upgrades, and separately purchased collections. It is especially strong in fast desktop search and original-language work.

Lesson: speed, precision, and durable access to purchased resources build trust. Users need clear ownership terms, exports, and a migration path.

### Olive Tree: contextual mobile study

[Olive Tree](https://www.olivetree.com/blog/apps/ios/) keeps the Bible visible while a Resource Guide follows the current passage. It supports offline reading, split-screen study, subscriptions, and individual resource purchases.

Lesson: the most useful library is the one that appears at the verse where it is needed. Mobile study should not require hunting through a catalog.

### Bible Gateway Plus: a simple premium layer

[Bible Gateway Plus](https://www.biblegateway.com/plus/) is currently $6.99 monthly or $69.99 annually. Its proposition is easy to explain: remove ads and attach a curated premium reference library to the passage being read.

Lesson: a modest subscription can work when the benefit is immediate, focused, and understandable.

### Blue Letter Bible: free depth supported by donors

[Blue Letter Bible](https://www.blueletterbible.org/iPad/whatBLB.cfm) provides free verse-centered lexical, commentary, cross-reference, sermon, hymn, and encyclopedia tools. Its published [finance and governance overview](https://www.blueletterbible.org/assets-v3/pdf/donor/Intro-Finance-Governing-BLB.pdf) emphasizes board oversight, audits, public reporting, and donor trust.

Lesson: a serious free study ministry can be sustained, but donation support requires visible impact, responsible governance, and financial transparency.

### YouVersion: habit, accessibility, and scale

[YouVersion](https://blog.youversion.com/getstarted/) succeeds through an approachable reader, audio, reading plans, saved progress, sharing, and daily habits. Its [2025 impact reporting](https://blog.youversion.com/2025/07/global-impact-update/) shows the scale possible when Scripture access stays free and donors fund broad distribution.

Lesson: depth alone does not create regular use. Plans, reminders, audio, saved progress, and a welcoming first session help people return to Scripture.

### Dwell: premium audio and church distribution

[Dwell](https://dwellapp.io/pricing) currently lists $9.99 monthly and $59.99 annual individual plans, while [church plans](https://dwellapp.io/churches?period=annual) scale with attendance. Dwell explains that subscriptions pay for development, voice talent, audio engineering, licensing, and scholarships through its [giving program](https://dwellapp.io/giving).

Lesson: excellent audio is a real product, not a free byproduct. Church distribution and scholarships can support both sustainability and access.

## Closure And Continuity Lessons

There is no responsible basis in the reviewed sources for saying BibleWorks or Wordsearch went bankrupt. The useful facts are narrower.

- [BibleWorks](https://www.bibleworks.com/) ceased operations in 2018 after 26 years, citing a variety of reasons. Its later activation and reactivation arrangements show the long tail created by installed software and activation dependencies.
- [Wordsearch](https://www.logos.com/wordsearch) was acquired by Faithlife in 2020 after reaching the end of its technical life. Users were offered Logos equivalents and library migration.

These cases point to product risks rather than a single financial explanation:

- Old platforms become expensive to maintain.
- Activation servers can threaten access to legitimately purchased resources.
- Closed data formats make transitions painful.
- Large catalogs carry continuing licensing, metadata, support, and migration obligations.
- A company needs a continuity plan before selling "permanent" digital access.

Father's Business should provide user data exports, portable notes, documented ownership terms, tested backups, and a succession or shutdown plan. Do not sell lifetime access early. If lifetime access is ever offered, its price must fund several years of expected service plus a protected reserve.

## The Product Workflow That Matters

The app should organize tools around one repeatable study session:

1. Read the KJV verse in its paragraph and chapter context.
2. Tap a word for a quick Webster 1828 definition and the linked Strong's entry when available.
3. Follow TSK cross references without losing the original passage.
4. Compare a small number of clearly labeled commentaries, with author, date, source, and rights status visible.
5. Record observations, interpretation, doctrinal connections, and application.
6. Write one prayer or specific act of obedience arising from the passage.
7. Save the study as a reusable lesson, sermon outline, presentation, or personal review card.

Efficiency features should support that path:

- one-tap word definitions inside the verse
- a passage-following study drawer
- fast reference navigation and search
- automatic note saving and clear exports
- 15-, 30-, and 60-minute guided study sessions
- source and rights evidence on every resource
- offline reading and previously downloaded study packs
- a review queue for memory verses, prior notes, and unfinished applications
- uncluttered mobile and tablet layouts

The radio, hymn, sermon, media, presentation, and marketplace ideas can become valuable differentiators, but they should follow the dependable read-study-save-teach workflow rather than delay it.

## Sustainable Revenue Design

### Free forever

- KJV Bible reading and search
- Webster 1828, Strong's, TSK, and reviewed public-domain references
- basic notes, bookmarks, reading progress, and study sessions
- public-domain hymns and basic playback where audio rights are verified
- basic sermon notes and presentation output
- rights and source information

### Supporter path now

Begin with optional donations or a $5-$10 monthly supporter contribution. Support should not quietly become payment for material that must remain free. Report what contributions fund: hosting, content review, permissions, accessibility, audio production, and support.

### Study Plus later

Suggested target: $6.99-$8.99 monthly or $69-$89 annually.

Only introduce this after the paid readiness checklist is complete. Possible benefits:

- reliable multi-device sync and version history
- advanced study workspaces and saved workflows
- enhanced search across personal notes and owned resources
- larger private storage and personal imports
- advanced audio queues and offline packs
- premium presentation themes, media organization, and exports
- original Father's Business study packs
- licensed resources whose contracts permit subscription access

### Preacher And Teacher later

Suggested target: $12.99-$16.99 monthly or $129-$169 annually.

Possible benefits:

- sermon and lesson archive
- teaching-team collaboration
- presentation remote and service planning
- church media library
- advanced exports and handouts
- metered high-cost features, including any future licensed AI assistance

### Church plan after individual retention

A small-church plan around $199-$499 annually could include a defined number of leaders, shared teaching resources, presentation controls, and member invitations. Institutional plans should wait until administration, privacy, support, and permissions are mature.

### Individual books and media

A marketplace can eventually sell properly licensed books, courses, audio, and study packs. Do not open it until contracts specify territory, formats, pricing control, revenue share, refunds, taxes, takedowns, updates, accessibility, AI/TTS rights, and what happens if the service closes. Keep purchased items visibly separate from subscription access.

## Realistic Funding Levels

These are planning estimates, not quotations. Founder compensation, licensing choices, insurance, and support volume can change them substantially.

### Level 1: founder-led public beta

Estimated first-year cash need: **$25,000-$75,000**, if the founder is not drawing a full salary.

- infrastructure and operational tools: approximately $75-$250 per month at early scale
- legal, accounting, insurance, policies, and initial rights review: approximately $5,000-$15,000
- part-time content review, accessibility, design, or engineering: approximately $1,000-$4,000 per month as needed
- contingency for migration, support, storage, and media processing

Current entry infrastructure prices support a low-cost beta: [Vercel Pro](https://vercel.com/pricing) starts at $20 per month, [Supabase Pro](https://supabase.com/pricing) starts at $25 per month, and [Cloudflare R2](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/) offers inexpensive object storage without egress charges. Usage can still make costs rise, especially for audio, video, search, email, and AI.

### Level 2: sustainable small public product

Estimated annual operating budget: **$180,000-$350,000**.

This can support roughly one full-time engineering/product role plus part-time content editing, customer support, legal/accounting, ministry outreach, and modest marketing. At $7 net recurring revenue per paying user per month, break-even requires approximately **2,143-4,167 paying equivalents**.

### Level 3: serious growth organization

Estimated annual operating budget: **$600,000-$1.2 million**.

This supports a small multidisciplinary team, native mobile work, licensed catalog operations, dependable support, content production, and sustained outreach. At $8 net recurring revenue per paying equivalent per month, break-even requires approximately **6,250-12,500 paying equivalents**.

Card processing must be included in net revenue. [Stripe's standard US pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing) currently lists 2.9% plus $0.30 for domestic card transactions, before any additional billing, international, dispute, tax, or currency costs.

### A mixed first-stage example

- 1,000 supporters or members averaging $7 net per month: $84,000 annually
- 100 churches at $249 annually: $24,900
- licensed book, study-pack, or affiliate net revenue: $25,000
- additional gifts and ministry partners: $50,000
- approximate annual total: **$183,900**

The mix reduces dependence on one subscription and preserves a meaningful free ministry. It is an illustration, not a forecast.

## Financial Guardrails

- Keep at least six months of operating cash before hiring permanent staff; aim for twelve months before making long-term access promises.
- Maintain separate accounting for donations, subscriptions, book sales, restricted gifts, sales tax, publisher royalties, and refunds.
- Set spending alerts and hard limits for hosting, storage, email, media delivery, and future AI usage.
- Measure contribution margin by plan instead of treating gross receipts as available cash.
- Do not use annual prepayments to hide a monthly operating loss.
- Publish a simple annual ministry and financial impact report if donations become material.
- Establish independent financial and rights oversight as the organization grows.

## Launch Gates And Metrics

Before a broad free beta:

- 100% of public library items have source and rights evidence
- production admin routes are inaccessible to ordinary and signed-out users
- backups and at least one full restore have been tested
- mobile reading, word lookup, notes, study, and presentation paths pass QA
- feedback and resource correction reports reach a monitored inbox or queue

Before charging a subscription:

- at least 100-250 active testers have used the product
- at least 25% of activated testers return in week four as an initial target
- at least 40% of weekly active testers complete one meaningful read-study-save action as an initial target
- sync conflict, export, refund, account deletion, and support workflows are tested
- support can answer normal requests within two business days
- users can clearly explain why they would pay without being prompted with a feature list

The retention percentages are internal targets, not universal industry benchmarks. Replace them with observed cohort data as soon as the beta has enough users.

## Recommended Sequence

1. Finish the inline Webster 1828 and Strong's word-definition experience.
2. Stabilize the complete read-study-save-teach workflow on phone, tablet, and desktop.
3. Add cloud sync, backup restore, export, account deletion, and support operations.
4. Launch a small free public beta with optional donations.
5. Measure weekly retention and interview users who prepare lessons and sermons.
6. Improve the few workflows people actually repeat.
7. Pilot one paid workflow tier with founding users.
8. Add church plans only after individual support is dependable.
9. Add licensed books and media only after contracts and royalty operations are ready.
10. Expand radio, presentation media, and premium audio after core retention funds their continuing cost.

## Decision

The app does not need Logos-sized capital to prove demand. A careful founder-led beta can operate within a $25,000-$75,000 first-year cash range, but a dependable public product with continuing content, support, and engineering should plan toward a $180,000-$350,000 annual organization.

The next proof is not the number of books in the catalog. It is whether serious Bible students repeatedly move from a KJV passage to sound study, a saved conclusion, and faithful teaching or obedience with less friction than before.
