# Permission Request Guide

## Purpose

Permission outreach should be easy to understand before it becomes legally detailed. The first
message asks one plain question about one exact resource. Detailed licensing terms come only after
the author, ministry, or publisher says it is open to the conversation.

## The Two-Stage Process

### Stage 1: Ask For Interest

Keep the first message under 150 words whenever practical. It should answer five questions:

1. Who are we?
2. What exact book, recording, video, or resource are we asking about?
3. What one thing do we want permission to do?
4. How would that help the rights holder and the reader?
5. Who is the correct person to discuss it with?

Use one plain request, such as:

> Would you be open to discussing permission for readers who purchase *[Title]* to read it inside
> Father's Business Bible Study beside the KJV passages it explains?

Do not put a contract checklist, technical architecture, every possible future feature, or a broad
catalog request in the first message. Do not use terms such as entitlement, authenticated delivery,
territory, perpetual access, or takedown unless the recipient asks for details.

### Stage 2: Define Written Permission

If the recipient is interested, offer a short demonstration and ask which business or rights person
should handle the details. Then document each requested right separately:

- exact title, edition, author, and rights holder
- official listing, description, cover, and sample excerpt
- full-text storage and purchaser-only reading
- Bible passage links and purchaser-only search
- audio streaming, embedding, download, editing, and transcription
- video streaming, embedding, download, editing, and transcription
- presentation, classroom, printing, and export use
- text-to-speech, AI narration, summaries, and derivative material
- price, purchase route, revenue share, reporting, refunds, and taxes
- territory, term, updates, security, withdrawal, and existing-customer access

Nothing is approved unless the written response or signed agreement identifies that exact use.
Permission for a cover does not grant book text. Permission for a book does not grant its audiobook.
Permission to link to media does not grant permission to copy, download, edit, or resell it.

## Ask For One Format At A Time

Use a single sentence that says exactly what the app would do:

- **Book:** "Would you be open to discussing permission for purchasers of *[Title]* to read the
  full book inside the app?"
- **Audio:** "Would you be open to discussing permission for the app to stream your recording
  *[Exact Title]* from files you authorize?"
- **Video:** "Would you be open to discussing permission for the app to show your video *[Exact
  Title]* through an official embed or another method you approve?"

Do not combine full text, audio, video, transcripts, artificial narration, resale, and an entire
catalog in the first request. A short first message is an invitation to discuss one use, not the
license itself.

## What A Usable Written Agreement Must Say

A friendly "that sounds fine" is useful interest but may not define enough rights to publish. The
final written permission or contract should identify:

1. the legal rights holder and the exact title, edition, recording, or video
2. whether the app may link, embed, stream, store, copy, sell, search, excerpt, transcribe, or edit
3. whether access is free, paid, or limited to verified purchasers
4. the approved price or revenue arrangement, reporting schedule, and payment route
5. where and how long the permission applies
6. required attribution, copyright notice, samples, security, updates, and removal process
7. what happens to access already purchased if the agreement ends

For a significant paid catalog, have a qualified publishing or intellectual-property attorney
review the final agreement before launch.

## Rights Holder Check

Before sending, confirm who controls each format. An author may control the manuscript while a
publisher controls the current edition, cover, eBook, or audiobook. A ministry may own a recording
without owning the music, photographs, quotations, or third-party material inside it.

For public-domain candidates, verify the exact edition and source. A public-domain hymn text does
not automatically make a modern arrangement or recording public domain.

## Initial Email Template

**Subject:** Question about one resource for a Bible study app

Dear [Name],

My name is Stephen Scheer, and I am building Father's Business Bible Study, a KJV-centered app for
Bible study, teaching, and sermon preparation.

Would you be open to discussing permission for readers who purchase *[Exact Title]* to read it
inside the app beside the KJV passages it explains? My hope is to direct readers to trustworthy
resources while the author or publisher keeps ownership and approves how the resource is offered.

I am not asking for broad catalog permission, and I will not copy or publish the resource without
written approval. If this is worth discussing, whom should I contact? I would be glad to show a
short private demonstration first.

Thank you,

Stephen Scheer
Father's Business Bible Study
hello@fathersbusinessmasteryresources.com
https://study.fathersbusinessmasteryresources.com/

## Send Checklist

- Name one exact resource and one exact proposed use.
- Use plain language and keep the initial request short.
- Verify the current official contact route.
- Check Inbox, Spam, Sent, and the outreach log to prevent duplicates.
- Never claim partnership, approval, availability, or endorsement before written permission.
- Record the exact recipient, subject, body, date, time, response, and approved rights.
- Keep doctrinal review separate from copyright and commercial permission.
