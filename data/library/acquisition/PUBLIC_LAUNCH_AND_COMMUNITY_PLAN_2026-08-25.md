# Public Launch and Community Plan

Prepared: 2026-08-25

Public beta target: Thursday, April 15, 2027 at 7:00 PM EDT

This plan supports an honest coming-soon campaign without announcing unfinished capabilities. The
public promise is a useful founding beta, not a finished replacement for every Logos, e-Sword, or
presentation feature.

## The Public Reason To Care

Father's Business Bible Study is a KJV-centered study, preparation, library, and presentation
workspace for pastors, teachers, families, and serious Bible students. Its clearest differentiators
are:

- one passage can gather KJV text, Webster's 1828 definitions, Strong's, cross-references,
  commentary, maps, hymns, illustrations, notes, and presentation material;
- resources are curated, labeled, source-traceable, and not treated as endorsed merely because they
  appear in the catalog;
- mobile reading, sermon and lesson preparation, and presentation are designed as one workflow;
- public-domain material is reviewed instead of bulk-imported blindly; and
- licensed resources will remain tied to exact written permissions and purchaser access.

Do not advertise “the safest,” “the best,” “a complete Logos replacement,” or any feature that has
not passed a real rehearsal. Show what works on screen and describe unfinished work as planned or in
development.

## Feedback Offer

Offer one original or fully verified public-domain study booklet to every person who completes the
private product-feedback survey. The offer must not depend on positive comments, a five-star review,
a testimonial, a social post, or a donation.

Recommended first offer: **The Passage Preparation Starter Book**, an original short guide that
teaches a repeatable KJV passage-study workflow and includes printable worksheets. Do not announce
the offer until the finished file, source notes, download route, and privacy notice are ready.

Ask these questions:

1. What ministry role or study setting best describes you?
2. What Bible-study task takes too long today?
3. Which three tools must be available beside a passage?
4. What would make you trust or distrust a curated resource library?
5. What must work on a phone or tablet?
6. What would make sermon or lesson presentation dependable enough to use publicly?
7. Which pricing model would feel fair for the core app and separately licensed books?
8. May we contact you for a 15-minute private beta interview?

If feedback is later quoted publicly, obtain separate permission and disclose any free product or
other material connection. FTC guidance does not allow an incentive to be conditioned on positive
sentiment and requires relevant material connections to be disclosed:
https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers

## Funding Path

The existing $50,000 founding-beta target is a one-year operating goal for engineering, testing,
content review, permissions, hosting, legal and accounting readiness, launch support, and reserve.
Keep the published budget visible and update the raised amount only from reconciled records.

Before collecting money:

1. decide whether payments are gifts, preorders, memberships, sponsorships, or purchases;
2. confirm the legal entity, bank account, bookkeeping categories, processor ownership, refund terms,
   privacy notice, receipts, and chargeback process;
3. have a qualified accountant and attorney review the public wording; and
4. never call a payment tax-deductible unless the receiving organization qualifies and the receipt
   process supports that statement.

If a supporter receives a book, subscription, or other substantial benefit, do not describe the
entire payment as a donation. Relevant IRS references:

- https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/charitable-contribution-deductions
- https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/charitable-contributions-quid-pro-quo-contributions

Until those controls are ready, collect **founding supporter interest**, not payment. Report progress
by completed milestones as well as dollars.

## Licensing Pipeline

Follow `PERMISSION_REQUEST_GUIDE.md` for every commercial resource:

1. identify the exact title, edition, format, and likely rights holder;
2. send a short first message asking about one title and one use;
3. show a private demonstration only after the recipient expresses interest;
4. define each right separately in writing;
5. complete doctrinal and usefulness review separately from rights review;
6. obtain approved source files and create passage-level markup without altering source wording;
7. test purchaser access, reporting, refunds, updates, and withdrawal before sale; and
8. have a qualified publishing or intellectual-property attorney review significant paid licenses.

Do not present royalty percentages as industry guarantees. Price, advance, minimum guarantee,
wholesale discount, revenue share, reporting, and taxes vary by title, format, rights holder, market,
and distribution model. Ask the rights holder what commercial model it uses, then negotiate and
record the actual terms.

## Public-Domain Correction

The “published before 1929” shortcut is out of date and too broad for operational use. As of 2026,
the U.S. Copyright Office states that U.S. works published before January 1, 1931 are in the public
domain, but exact status can still depend on publication history, edition, territory, underlying
works, and separately protected translation, editing, artwork, arrangement, recording, or scan.

Use the Copyright Office duration and research guidance rather than a fixed folklore date:

- https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ15a.pdf
- https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ22.pdf

instead of shipping a public promise that cannot yet be supported.

## Measures That Matter

- percentage of new users who complete a passage study;
- percentage who return within seven days;
- time from opening a passage to saving useful notes;
- successful mobile sessions and presentation rehearsals;
- corrections resolved with source evidence;
- publisher conversations, demonstrations, written permissions, and licensed titles as separate
  counts; and
- monthly operating cost, restricted obligations, revenue, refunds, and runway.

Followers, views, and email signups matter only when they lead to useful study, trustworthy feedback,
or sustainable support.
