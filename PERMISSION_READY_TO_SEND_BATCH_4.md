## When To Ask For A Whole Catalog

Do not make a whole-catalog request in an unanswered introduction. Use this progression:

1. **Introduction:** one exact title, official metadata and purchase link, one approved cover, and one approved excerpt.
2. **Title pilot:** after a positive reply, define purchaser access, search, Scripture/topic indexing, price, reporting, attribution, updates, and withdrawal for that title.
3. **Catalog conversation:** ask only after the first title's rights are clear and the partner has seen the workflow, or immediately if the rights holder expressly invites a broader proposal.
4. **Catalog agreement:** use a written title schedule or approved catalog feed. It must say which editions and formats are included; books, audio, video, images, transcripts, presentation use, and TTS remain separate rights unless expressly included.

A successful pilot is the evidence for the catalog request: accurate attribution, dependable purchaser access, correct sales reports and payments, responsive support, and prompt correction or removal. For a significant paid catalog, obtain publishing or intellectual-property counsel before launch.

Suggested catalog-transition wording:

> Thank you for allowing us to begin with *[Pilot Title]*. Once that pilot is working to your satisfaction, would you be open to discussing a title schedule or catalog feed for other works you select? You would retain ownership and approval, and we would document each included edition, format, price, permitted use, reporting requirement, and removal process in writing.
